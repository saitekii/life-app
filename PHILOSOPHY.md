# Why this app exists

This app was built for a specific problem: getting unstuck.

Not productivity. Not habit formation. Not self-optimization. Just the moment when you're frozen — overwhelmed, drifting, or paralyzed by the gap between where you are and where you think you should be — and you need the smallest possible thing to do next.

---

## What it is

A behavioral activation tool. The core insight of behavioral activation is simple: action precedes motivation, not the other way around. You don't wait until you feel ready to move. You move, and then you feel ready. The app is built around that idea. It tries to locate the smallest action that feels possible right now and offer it without judgment.

It has three entry points depending on what you need:

**Soft Reset** is for when you're stuck. It gives you one micro-action drawn from four tiers of decreasing friction — from small environmental shifts (stand up, open a window) down to the absolute floor (take one breath, just be here). Rejecting a suggestion always moves toward something simpler, never harder. The goal isn't to do that specific thing — the goal is motion in some direction.

**Life Areas** is for when you want to see what's actually going on. It walks through twelve domains of wellbeing one at a time and asks, simply: thriving, okay, or neglected? No judgment, no goals. The purpose is noticing. Sometimes the areas that have been quietly going hungry only become visible when you name them.

**What Needs You** is for when you have time and energy but no direction. It looks at what's been neglected across your check-in history and suggests one meaningful thing — not a micro-action, but something worth an hour. It points your momentum somewhere rather than leaving you with it and nowhere to go.

---

## What it isn't

It is not a productivity system. There are no tasks, no backlogs, no recurring reminders, no streaks, no completion rates. Nothing accumulates. Nothing goes overdue. Nothing judges you for absence.

It is not a habit tracker. Habits require repetition over time with consequences for failure. This app has no concept of failure. There is no version of using it incorrectly.

It is not motivational. The copy doesn't tell you that you can do it, that you're doing great, or that you should believe in yourself. That register is dishonest when someone is genuinely stuck. The tone is a calm friend, not a coach.

---

## The non-negotiable constraints

These aren't stylistic choices — they're load-bearing walls:

**No streaks.** Streaks transform a tool for getting unstuck into a source of guilt the moment you miss a day. For someone who already struggles with shame and self-blame, a broken streak is actively harmful. The app treats every return as a first visit.

**No overdue states.** Nothing in the app can be late. There is no backlog. The moment something can be "behind," the app starts producing anxiety instead of relieving it.

**Soft Reset always suggests exactly one thing.** Offering multiple options reintroduces the decision problem the app is supposed to solve. One thing, take it or find something easier.

**The weighting is invisible.** What you mark as neglected in your check-ins shapes which Soft Reset suggestions surface more often — areas with consistent neglect get more weight. But this is never shown to the user. The moment the system explains its reasoning, it becomes a system to analyze and second-guess rather than something to just use.

**Returning after weeks away feels identical to day one.** This is non-negotiable. People who struggle with getting started often have long gaps. If the app makes absence feel bad — through any mechanism — it becomes one more thing to avoid.

---

## On the twelve life areas

The twelve areas aren't a comprehensive taxonomy of human experience. They're a set of domains that, in practice, tend to go quietly neglected when someone is operating in survival mode. The categories — Foundations, Connection, Growth, Meaning, Joy & Vitality, Rhythm & Memory — were chosen to surface the kinds of needs that are easy to dismiss as non-urgent until they've been depleted for a long time.

The check-in deliberately doesn't ask you to rate all twelve simultaneously. One at a time. The cognitive load of seeing everything at once tends to produce either numbness or overwhelm. Seeing one area at a time produces honesty.

---

## On friction

Everything in the app is designed around the idea that for someone who is already overwhelmed, any additional friction is a reason not to start. This is especially true for ADHD, where the gap between intending to do something and initiating it can be enormous and has nothing to do with effort or motivation.

The tier system in Soft Reset exists because the "right" friction level isn't fixed — it depends on the day, the hour, the state you're in. What feels manageable at 10am may be impossible at midnight. Moving to a lower tier isn't giving up. It's accurate calibration.

---

## On the philosophy behind it

Two ideas shaped this app more than anything else:

**Behavioral activation** — the clinical insight that depression and overwhelm create an avoidance cycle where inaction reinforces low mood, and the way out is small action. Not forced positivity. Just doing something, anything, and letting the doing change the state.

**Hard determinism** — the understanding that behavior is the product of biology, environment, and history, not pure agency. This isn't fatalism. It's a reason for compassion — toward yourself and others. The app tries to embody this: it never implies you should be doing better, or that you'd be doing better if you just tried harder. It meets you where you are.

The app also draws on stoic practice — specifically the idea that you can only control your response to what arises, not what arises itself. Soft Reset doesn't ask you to fix anything. It asks what's in your hands right now, and suggests the smallest version of that.
